BearWave Borneo — retrospective analysis protocol, amendment v1.3
6 October 2026

1. STATUS AND OBJECTIVE

This is retrospective analysis, developed after the April 2023 field trial and
inspection of its results. It is not a preregistration or a claim that all
comparisons were planned before data collection. It preserves the numerical
reception rules of v1.0/v1.2 and documents later exploratory analyses and author
clarifications. It does not imply supervisor approval.

The objective is field communication feasibility for sparse conservation
messages. The four manuscript research questions remain unchanged. The measured
outcomes are reception occurrence, decoded SNR/DT and within-window reception
spacing. Reliability, coverage, energy autonomy and welfare outcomes are distinct
evaluation objectives.

Earlier manuscript results were selected partly to support the intended
argument. The current numerical analysis includes the complete identified
trial-sender receive inventory from the supplied archive: 1,177 unique rows,
comprising 484 previously selected receptions and 693 additional receptions.
The earlier spreadsheet also contained 17 local transmissions, excluded from
receive analysis. Original-selection and additional-record summaries remain
available, so the previous selection can be assessed rather than hidden.

The full identified inventory is not proof that every field observation or
attempted transmission was recorded. Every analysed reception is retained;
poor SNR, long spacing and known equipment incidents are not exclusion criteria.

2. RECEPTION INPUT AND EARLIER RECONSTRUCTION

inputs/receptions.csv matches the measurement rows published at the GitHub
commit in provenance.json. One row is a decoded receive record, not necessarily
a complete application message, independent trial or acknowledged event.

The earlier reconstruction assigned frequency from internal tuning headers,
carried forward in original file order; the author confirmed software-controlled
tuning. Filenames do not determine band. Exact duplicate backup records were
consolidated, preserving source references, and local transmissions/other senders
were excluded from the target-sender analysis. The current package starts AFTER
those steps; original message content and raw logs are not included, so it does
not independently revalidate raw sender classification or duplicate identity.

Timestamps in the reception data are UTC, confirmed by the author as GPS-set.
SNR, DT and audio offset retain their source values. No smoothing, imputation,
outcome trimming or failed-message SNR substitution is used. All current rows
have finite SNR and DT. Malformed source material is an upstream reconstruction
matter, not silently treated as zero in this analysis.

3. OBSERVATION WINDOWS AND ELIGIBILITY

The fixed W1–W7 partition is reproduced from inputs/window_rules.csv:
W1: 5.357 MHz, UTC dates 17–19 April; W2: 7.078 MHz, 18 April;
W3: 7.078 MHz, 20–21 April; W4: 10.130 MHz, 23–24 April;
W5: 5.357 MHz, 25–26 April; W6: 10.130 MHz, 25–27 April;
W7: 5.357 MHz, 27 April.

Date-rule starts are inclusive and ends exclusive. These rules reproduce the
existing retrospective partition, rather than a previously unrecorded operating
schedule. Every eligible input row must match exactly one rule; unmatched or
multiply matched rows cause validation to fail. No row is selected on SNR.

W7 separates a day with changed test cadence. Its UTC midnight boundary is a
reporting choice, not a verified radio-setting change. Different-frequency
windows can overlap. Table II first/last times are observed reception bounds,
not operating start/stop times. The windows may span site and power changes.

The timing amendment treats the author's breakdown times as approximate. All
explicit local diary times convert at UTC+8; the receive records themselves are
not shifted. Dates, identified hardware and nominal powers in
inputs/current_configuration.csv follow the current manuscript. No precise
per-record configuration is fabricated across approximate change times.

No target-sender receptions were identified at 3.578 or 14.078 MHz. Their absence
from positive-reception windows does not create a failed-attempt denominator;
the manuscript reports these observations and the 24 April repeat-setting issue.
The 3.578 MHz value follows the author's instruction to use receiver tuning.

4. RECEPTION SUMMARY RULES

Report counts and SNR/DT minimum, type-7 quartiles, median and maximum for each
window, pooled frequency and full inventory. Type-7 uses sorted position
(n-1)*p and linear interpolation. Empty subsets have n=0 and blank statistics.

Keep all, test-only (source category CQ), test plus heartbeat, heartbeat-only,
identifier-only, prior-selection, additional, prior-test and additional-test
summaries. The original category name is preserved for traceability; the paper
calls these controlled test messages. Summary spreads are not confidence
intervals. SNR is conditional on successful decoding and is not received power.

Within each window, sort test records by timestamp and stable ID. Subtract
consecutive timestamps, retaining zero spacings if present; n receptions yield
n-1 intervals. Report every tied mode. Do not fill gaps or derive missed attempts
from the nominal test interval. The longest interval excludes the periods before
the first reception, after the last and between windows, and is not a worst-case
field-trial outage or an alarm-delivery bound.

Nominal-1-W associations use the trial report's chart1, chart3 and chart4 cached
numeric points. Convert fractional-day axes to seconds with nearest-second
rounding and advance the date when the plotted clock wraps; dates come from
chart/report labels. Match timestamp, frequency and SNR exactly to one receive
row. The reported 126/102/92 associations are chart subsets, not simply the
previously selected spreadsheet rows. The matching validates numerical lineage;
the nominal power itself remains a documentary assignment.

5. EXPLORATORY TIME-OF-DAY ANALYSIS

Added after examination of the results, following discussion of diurnal
behaviour. Convert UTC to local UTC+8 and group ALL test records by original
window, local calendar date and fixed four-hour bins starting at 00,04,08,12,16,20.
Report n, median, minimum and maximum for every populated bin. Empty bins are
absent, not zero-SNR measurements. No other dates/bands/bins are discarded.

The manuscript illustrates afternoon 12:00–15:59 versus evening 20:00–23:59 in
both 7.078 MHz ridge trials. This is a post hoc descriptive comparison; no
significance test, independent-sample assumption or causal ionospheric effect
is asserted. Local interference can also influence decoder SNR.

6. IONOSONDE AND MAP INPUTS

The workbook's Darwin J21:J1364 and Guam K21:K1364 columns are labelled 2023
in row 20. Month/day and clock come from columns A/B; A stores a common older
year, so the explicit series-header year supplies 2023. Each station has 1,344
quarter-hour observations from 15–28 April. The author confirmed UTC, MHz, and
direct copying from the download without additional averaging or gap filling.
Source quality flags and original download files are not included. Figure 1
plots the supplied values directly, without smoothing or interpolation.

The maps are Bureau of Meteorology global foF2 estimates, not local Borneo
ionograms. The 667 PNGs span 14 April 12:30 to 21 April 14:00 UTC, with 12 absent
scheduled maps inside that extent. There is no supplied coverage for 23–27 April.
Map filename timestamps are used; representative rendered title dates were
checked, not every image title independently OCR-verified.

The equirectangular plot bounds are x=31..541, y=33..431 in 640x479 images. The
field-centre location 5.413643 N,118.037809 E maps to pixel (453,220). Original
RGB pixels from a 7x7 patch and legend colours for classes 1..14 MHz are included.
The exact extraction geometry, source image hashes and palette are recorded.

For each 3x3,5x5,7x7 patch, retain exact palette-colour matches. The unique modal
class is the estimate; ties/no matches remain undefined. Main results use 5x5;
3x3 and 7x7 test sensitivity. Non-palette pixels are not assigned the nearest
colour. Coastline overlays, raster resolution and class discretisation limit
physical precision; the classes are approximate estimates, not local soundings.

Match each reception to the nearest scheduled quarter-hour within 7.5 minutes;
an exact midpoint uses the earlier slot. If that slot's map is missing, leave
the reception unmatched rather than choosing a farther image. Keep matched rows
even when their map mode is unresolved. This yields 392 matched receptions,
379 of them test records; matching does not demonstrate the propagation path.

Daily profiles use six complete local calendar days, 15–20 April inclusive.
Compute pooled hourly and 04–06,10–17,18–23 block medians from available resolved
map classes, without equal-day reweighting or interpolation. Sensitivity results
use the same date/block rules for each patch size. A +/-1 MHz comparison buffer
is a graphical sensitivity convention, not an error bar or confidence interval.

Darwin/Guam and the global maps share observational inputs including GIRO;
agreement is contextual evidence, not independent validation. Higher foF2 does
not itself explain absence of lower-frequency decoding, and map comparisons do
not select a proven optimum operating frequency or prove pure NVIS propagation.

7. CHAMBER DIAGNOSTIC

The supplied text contains Pi output, not the chamber's raw temperature logger.
Skip repeated column headers and blank lines; parse all other rows explicitly.
Preserve time-of-day without inventing a time zone. Decode the binary status
word's current/history undervoltage and throttling bits separately. Historical
flags do not identify the time of the original event. Do not count every flagged
sample as an independent incident.

The 26 October 2023 date, 30 C chamber setpoint and +/-0.1 C stability are author-
supplied context. The battery was inside the chamber; the Pi4/Hermes Lite system
ran SparkSDR and JS8Call with a dummy load and no live over-the-air receptions.
The Pi series reproduces the 1,930 samples and 58.4–75.0 C range. It does not
independently verify chamber stability or establish the cause of field crashes.

8. TARGETS, FIGURES AND CHANGE CONTROL

The current notification design scenario is ten hours within a conditional
twelve-hour attendance allowance, assuming two hours for staff mobilisation
and travel. Earlier eight-hour target statements are historical and superseded.
This is not a universal sun-bear confinement limit or measured deadline result.
Thirty-day autonomy and reduced travel/risk/cost are system targets or intended
benefits, not measurements inferred from successful decodes.

The distribution figure's complete numerical coordinate list is checked against
recomputed summaries. The station-figure data are checked against source series.
The solar-noise image remains qualitative; no absolute noise calibration or
before/after attenuation estimate is created from its colours.

Expected outputs are frozen comparison targets, not calculation inputs. The
file manifest detects accidental source/expected-result changes. Preserve the
historical public release and record any future amendments explicitly. No
receipt rows or paper statistics were changed by this reproducibility work.
